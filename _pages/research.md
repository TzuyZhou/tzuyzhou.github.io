Currently, I am working on the $$(1+1)$$ dimensional quantum [**sine-Gordon field theory**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine-Gordon_equation), which has attracted tremendous attention for its properties related to the *relativity* and *integrability*. It has been considered as one of the prototypical interacting quantum field theories that can be studied in depth using non-perturbative methods.

In classical mechanics, integrability is characterized by the existence of a sufficient number of independent conserved quantities that are in involution with respect to the Poisson bracket. This notion has a natural extension to field theories within the Hamiltonian formalism. In the quantum regime, integrability manifests itself in a particularly fascinating way through the highly constrained structure of scattering processes between asymptotic particles. In the sine-Gordon theory, for instance, the scattering matrix satisfies the **[Yang-Baxter equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Baxter_equation)**, which places strong constraints on the structure of the scattering matrix and, consequently, on the physical observables of the theory.

My current work is based on the so-called form-factor **bootstrap**. More specifically, rather than first choosing a particular quantization prescription (e.g. the path-integral formalism) and then calculating correlation functions and the $$S$$-matrix, I would like to explore the possibility of constructing a quantum field theory in the reverse direction: starting from a set of axioms governing the properties of form factors, together with an explicit expression for the $$S$$-matrix, can we establish the existence of a quantum field theory satisfying all the desired principles such as [Wightman axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wightman_axioms)? 

> Apart from my main research direction, I also have an interest in statistical physics and probability theory, which have played some important roles in the development of ideas and methods related to my research.
