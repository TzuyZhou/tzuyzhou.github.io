I am a PhD student in mathematical physics at the [Laboratoire de Physique, École Normale Supérieure de Lyon (ENS de Lyon)](https://www.ens-lyon.fr/PHYSIQUE), with a focus on integrable quantum field theory. Generally speaking, my work aims at the mathematically rigorous construction of this special class of quantum field theories in a $$(1+1)$$-dimensional spacetime, with the ultimate goal of deepening our understanding of real world through these toy models. More specifically, my work exploits advanced mathematical methods and the physical properties associated with integrability to tackle the technical challenges that arise in the construction of integrable quantum field theories. For more details, please see the [Research](/research/) section.

Before coming to France, I obtained my MSc degree from the [Department of Physics, ETH Zürich](https://www.phys.ethz.ch), where my research interests shifted from more experimentally oriented topics in particle physics towards aspects and methods of modern mathematical physics. In particular, I have been drawn to integrable models for their exceptionally rich symmetry structures, and I completed my master's thesis on the relation between the integrable structures of classical integrable models under different boundary conditions.

Although I am currently working as a theorist, my journey in physics began from a more experiment-oriented perspective. During my BSc at the [School of Physics and Technology, Wuhan University](https://physics.whu.edu.cn), driven by a strong curiosity about the nature of elementary particles, I completed several research projects in experimental and phenomenological particle physics. My work primarily focused on the analysis of Monte Carlo events, with particular attention to rare decay channels of charmed mesons and the sensitivity of dark matter models at colliders. This experience also provided me with a solid foundation in computer programming and computational techniques.
